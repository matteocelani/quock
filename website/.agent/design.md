# Quock website: design philosophy

This file explains **why** the site looks, reads and moves the way it does. The exact values live in code, so there is one home per value: tokens in `src/app/globals.css`, components in `src/components/ui/`. Read this before designing anything; skim the code before building it.

---

## The idea

Quock's promise is in its headline: *Open. Type. That's it.* The app removes every step between you and an answer. The website keeps that promise: it removes every step between a visitor and understanding the app. Each choice below follows from that.

## Six principles

1. **The product tells the story.** A visitor should *see* Quock before reading about it. So the hero is a working playground, every phone is a real capture, and the copy only names what the picture already shows. If a section could exist without its visual, it is a paragraph in the FAQ, not a section.

2. **One idea per screen.** Each section makes one claim: one headline, one visual, one supporting line. Big type and generous space are how we say "this matters". If a section needs two headlines, it is two sections.

3. **Calm surface, one accent.** The page is quiet: a soft blue-white tint (it echoes the app), ink-black type, and pale panels instead of borders. Colour goes to the highlighter stroke on one phrase per headline, to the wallpapers behind the phones, and inside a mock to the one active thing (the chosen line, the selected action), the way the app itself marks it. Links carry the accent as an underline. A highlight is an accent; too many of them turn into noise.

4. **Native, not decorated.** The site borrows its feel from iOS itself: the system font, glass only where something floats over imagery, springy presses, rounded-rectangle buttons, and a light/dark split that sets the reading pages apart. We add nothing iOS wouldn't: no gradient text on body copy, no drop shadows on flat UI, no ornaments.

5. **Positive and plain.** We say what Quock *is* ("independent, open source, your chats stay on your phone"), never what it isn't. Privacy is the one place where an absence is the feature, so "no analytics" is fine there, but lead with what stays yours. Sentences are short and end with a period. The one legal line lives quietly in the footer and the FAQ.

6. **Light enough to keep.** Every element has to justify its maintenance cost. The rules that follow from that:
   - no webfonts, no image CDN, no analytics;
   - two small client components, and CSS for everything else;
   - content kept as data in each feature's module (`src/modules/`);
   - captures regenerated by one script.

   Before adding something, try removing something. A site that is easy to update stays accurate, and an accurate site is the most persuasive one.

## How a page flows

The home page reads like a short conversation with someone deciding whether to try the app:

1. **Promise.** The H1 and the download, in the first screen.
2. **Proof.** The stage: touch it, and it behaves like the app.
3. **Depth.** Two showcases (models, then thinking). Each focuses on one capability and has tabs that play by themselves.
4. **Breadth.** "Every corner, covered.": small tiles for everything else, each with a tiny honest mock.
5. **Ease.** How it works, in four steps, linked to the full guide.
6. **Recap.** The icon grid: eight capabilities you can take in at a glance.
7. **Act.** The download again, then a quiet footer.

The inner pages (FAQ, changelog, guide, privacy) are for reading. They are dark and set in one narrow column with a big title and a list. Nothing on them competes with the text.

## Visual language, in brief

- **Type:**
  - one family, the system font, with weight 700 for headings and labels. Mock UI follows iOS: bold for labels, semibold for secondary text, and model names in the system monospace, as the app shows them;
  - a steep scale: a 96px hero, 70px sections, 36px subsections, 30px feature titles, a 23px lead, 24px list items, a 17px body (`text-body`), 15px UI copy (`text-ui`), 13px mock UI (`text-mock`), 11px chips (`text-chip`) and 10px tags (`text-tag`);
  - tight tracking on large sizes.
- **Colour:**
  - tint, ink, panel, muted and faint, plus a marker colour and a link accent;
  - light tokens on the home page, dark tokens on the inner pages;
  - colour never carries meaning on its own.
- **Shape:** buttons are rounded rectangles, 60px tall with a 16px radius by default; the `md` size is 52px for compact spots (the header, the privacy link) and the 44px icon-only button uses 12px. Secondary is a colour, not a size. Tiles are 24px, stages 40px at every size, app icons use the squircle token, and tags and chips use small radii. Pills are for the segmented control and iOS controls that are pills in the app (switches, a Continue button).
- **Depth:**
  - flat by default;
  - one raised shadow on the primary button;
  - glass only for things floating over moving content: panels over wallpaper, and the header once the page scrolls under it;
  - phones cast their own shadow.
- **Motion:**
  - things arrive by lifting and un-blurring, sections reveal as you scroll, and presses spring;
  - no endless motion: wallpapers are still, the thinking word sweeps twice (under 5s), and tab autoplay cycles only until the first tap, click, key or focus in its section, then stops for good (hover pauses it);
  - `prefers-reduced-motion` stops all of it, including tab autoplay.
- **Mock surfaces:** an opaque `surface` card with `shadow-card`, ink from the theme tokens, and the iOS Increase Contrast system colours (`system-red`, `system-yellow`, `system-green`) only where the real UI shows them, such as a PDF tag, a switch or window controls, so controls keep 3:1. Glass is for things floating over wallpaper, never over a card.
- **Imagery:**
  - real captures in the real iPhone frame;
  - wallpapers drawn in CSS;
  - small mock surfaces in HTML (menus, notifications, tables) that describe real app behaviour, never invented features; their sample text lives in the home module like any other copy.

## Voice

| Element | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Section headline | On the home page: short, ends with a period, can turn a phrase, at most one highlighted phrase. | "Every corner, covered." |
| Page title | Inner pages use a plain label, with no period. | "FAQs", "Changelog" |
| Lead | One bold sentence, dark rather than muted. Used under home headlines and at the top of reading pages. | "Pick any model on Ollama Cloud and start typing." |
| Tile copy | A bold lead-in, then a muted line. | "**Never lose an answer.** A reply cut short continues…" |
| Feature title | Two lines, no body text. | "Thinking / on demand" |
| Caption | Small, faint, factual. | "iOS 15.1+ · Free · Sign in with ollama.com" |
| Aside | At most one playful line per page. | "Psst… it's interactive!" |

"Ollama Cloud" is always what Quock connects to, never Quock itself. Model names are spelled exactly as the app shows them.

## Building something new

1. **Write the headline first.** If it needs a "but", it is two sections.
2. **Reuse a pattern** before inventing one: hero, stage, showcase, bento tile, numbered steps, icon grid, or an inner-page list. If you do invent one, add a line about it here.
3. **Build it from tokens and existing components.** A new value goes into `globals.css`, not into the markup.
4. **Check it at 390px and 1440px**, in both themes if it appears on both, with reduced motion on.
5. **Ask whether the page got simpler.** If it didn't, cut something.
